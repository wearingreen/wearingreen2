A Modern Approach To Brewing Very Lightly Roasted Coffee: Meet the Zuppa Lunga

Very lightly roasted coffee, such as Nordic-style coffee or lighter roasts, does not brew well as espresso using the "standard" 9 bars that are often featured on espresso machines. This is because light-roasted coffees are harder to extract due to their density, solubility, and thermal demands. If we treat the coffee like normal espresso, it will always be sour, astringent, lacking clarity, and muting the coffee's potential. Many enthusiasts try to adapt to this fact by grinding finer, increasing water temperature, and extracting longer, but this method falls short of what one can truly achieve with lightly roasted espresso. What we recommend to achieve maximum clarity, acidity, and terroir of a coffee on espresso is to ask you to forget everything you know about espresso. This is where Zuppa, and its longer version, Zuppa Lunga, comes into play. Zuppa, meaning soup in Italian, is a modern method of brewing lightly roasted coffee. Soup is an acronym for spro (espresso) only, unpressurized. It involves lower pressure, grinding coarser, and having a higher liquid-to-coffee ratio. When one brews with this method, channeling is decreased, extraction is pleasant, and it leaves you with a high clarity profile and an incredible mouthfeel. A phenomenal brewer that we use all the time at SLBC is not quite an espresso machine, but it creates soup; it's a weird in-between and pretty ambiguous as to what it exactly is, but we love it. It's called the OXO Rapid Brewer. Brewing on an OXO, you would typically aim for a 1:3-4 ratio (this is where Lunga comes in now); however, here we brew with a ratio of 1:10, which results in something that is almost in the realm of a pour-over but with more intensity, more punch, and of course, more clarity. A typical recipe of ours would be as follows:


  • 22g of coffee ground between espresso and moka
  • 220g of water at 89-93 °C
  • Grind coffee and set water. After the coffee is ground, put one paper filter in the coffee basket, place coffee on top, tamp, and set another filter down, creating a puck sandwich.
  • Assemble the brewer over a carafe with something in the basket on top of the puck to secure that top filter.
  • Pour your 22g of water into the water chamber, screw on the pump, and then slowly push down the pump over and over until you hear a hiss rather than the normal chop motion one would do with soup.
  • Enjoy.
